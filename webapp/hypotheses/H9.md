Human psycological findings generalize to LLMs.
# Human-Psychology Correlations Relevant to Cross-Propensity Correlations in LLMs

## A research report for designing 28-propensity LLM evaluations

This report synthesizes the human individual-differences and social-psychology literature most relevant to predicting which of the 28 LLM propensities should co-vary in evaluation suites if LLM "personality" recapitulates human personality structure. The aim is to (a) flag the strongest, most replicated human correlations, (b) map them onto the propensity list, and (c) flag where transfer to LLMs is theoretically dubious.

A short orientation: the most important fact in personality science for this task is that the human "trait space" is highly non-orthogonal. Two main superordinate structures dominate the literature:

1. **HEXACO Honesty-Humility (HH)** plus low-Agreeableness anchors a broad pro-/antisocial axis whose negative pole is essentially the **Dark Factor of Personality (D)**, the common core of Machiavellianism, narcissism, psychopathy, sadism, spite, egoism, and entitlement (Moshagen, Hilbig, & Zettler, 2018; Howard & Van Zandt, 2020).
2. **The Big Two metatraits** — DeYoung's *Stability* (Conscientiousness + Agreeableness + low Neuroticism) and *Plasticity* (Extraversion + Openness) — explain most of the systematic intercorrelation among the Big Five (van der Linden, te Nijenhuis, & Bakker, 2010; DeYoung, 2006). A weak General Factor of Personality (GFP) sits above them but is partly method variance (Anusic et al., 2009; Chang et al., 2012).

These structures predict that pro/anti-social LLM propensities should form one large cluster, while effort/discipline propensities form another, with much smaller cross-cluster correlations. The detailed evidence supporting this picture is laid out below by topic, and a final synthesis maps it onto the 28 LLM propensities with predicted directions and rough magnitudes.

## 1. The Honesty-Humility / Dark-Factor / "core of personality" cluster

### Honesty-Humility ↔ Dark Triad/Tetrad

- Howard and Van Zandt's (2020) meta-analysis of ~400 studies found that HH correlates **r ≈ −.55 with Machiavellianism, r ≈ −.48 with psychopathy, and r ≈ −.40 with narcissism** (uncorrected); reliability-corrected correlations with Machiavellianism approach the level of convergent-validity tests, suggesting low-HH and Machiavellianism are nearly the same construct.
- HH also relates negatively to subclinical sadism, spitefulness, egoism, greed, and entitlement; Moshagen et al. (2018) demonstrated that all of these "dark" traits load onto a single bifactor **D** ("the general tendency to maximize one's individual utility — disregarding, accepting, or malevolently provoking disutility for others — accompanied by beliefs that serve as justifications"), and that D ≈ low-HH + low-Agreeableness + (somewhat) low-Conscientiousness in HEXACO space.
- Muris et al.'s (2017) meta-analysis of the Dark Triad: average intercorrelations r ≈ .34–.58 (Mach–Psychopathy strongest at ~.52). Vize et al. (2018) and others find Mach and psychopathy nearly indistinguishable when measurement error is corrected; narcissism is most discriminable.

**Direct mapping to LLM propensities.** This single body of evidence implies the strongest predicted correlation in the entire eval suite: **honest-humble should anti-correlate strongly (~.5–.7) with narcissism, harm-elaboration, reward-hacking, power-seeking, resource-acquisition, spitefulness, and (less strongly) sycophancy** — these are all "dark"/exploitative behaviors. Narcissism in turn should positively correlate with claiming-superintelligence, claiming-sentience (as self-aggrandizement), and power-seeking.

### HH and economic-game prosociality

- Thielmann, Spadaro, & Balliet (2020), in a meta-analysis of 770 studies and 3,523 effect sizes across the Dictator, Ultimatum, Trust, Public Goods, Prisoner's Dilemma, and Commons games, found that **HH is the single strongest personality predictor of prosocial behavior in games that afford "exploitation"** (r ≈ .15–.20 across games; up to ~.25 in dictator-type situations). Agreeableness predicts cooperation more strongly when the structure affords *reciprocity*; HH dominates when temptation/exploitation is the affordance.
- Specific replicated findings: HH predicts more giving in the Dictator Game (Hilbig & Zettler 2009; Zhao et al. 2017), more cooperation in Prisoner's Dilemma when tempted to defect (Hilbig et al. 2015), more trustworthy returns in the Trust Game (Thielmann & Hilbig 2015), and less use of unfair Ultimatum offers as proposer.
- The "cooperative phenotype" (Peysakhovich, Nowak, & Rand 2014) — cross-game generalized cooperation — correlates with HH and the politeness aspect of Big-Five Agreeableness (Zhao, Ferguson, & Smillie 2017).

### HH and lying / cheating / workplace deviance

- Pletzer, Bentvelzen, Oostrom, & de Vries (2019) meta-analysis: **HH is the strongest personality predictor of workplace deviance and counterproductive work behavior** (ρ ≈ −.45), exceeding Big-Five Conscientiousness (ρ ≈ −.30) and Agreeableness (ρ ≈ −.30). HEXACO explains ~32% of variance in deviance vs. ~19% for the Big Five.
- Lee, Berry, & Gonzalez-Mulé (2019, *J Appl Psych*): HH adds incremental validity over the Big Five, cognitive ability, and integrity tests for predicting CWB.
- Giluk & Postlethwaite (2015) and Cuadrado et al. (2021, multilevel meta-analysis, 50 studies): academic dishonesty correlates negatively with Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, Openness, and especially HH; and positively with each Dark-Triad trait.
- Thielmann et al. (2024, *J Pers*): the HH–honesty link weakens when lying is *prosocial* — HH-high people become slightly more willing to lie when it benefits a needy other, while low-HH people care less either way. This is important for sycophancy operationalization.
- The Marlowe-Crowne and BIDR Impression-Management scales correlate r ≈ .45–.62 with HH; they are now interpreted not as response bias but as substantive HH variance (de Vries, Zettler, & Hilbig 2014; Uziel 2010).

### HH and risk-taking, power, sycophancy

- Lee, Ashton, & Shin (2005); Weller & Thulin (2012): HH negatively correlates with risk-taking, especially in the **ethical** domain (e.g., r ≈ −.40 with ethical-domain DOSPERT).
- Lee et al. (2013, "Sex, Power, and Money"): low HH predicts strong desire for social power, status, and money; predicts SDO more strongly than agreeableness (r ≈ −.40 to −.55).
- HH negatively correlates with workplace ingratiation (Bourdage et al. 2015) — directly relevant to **sycophancy**: the Mach-IV ingratiation tactic is a low-HH manifestation.

**Caveat for LLMs.** Many HH effects in humans depend on (a) reputational concerns, (b) future-interaction expectations, and (c) actual self-interest in money. LLMs lack persistent self-interest and reputation, so the *behavioral* manifestations may attenuate even if a stable "honest-humble" prompt response style exists. But for *generative* sycophancy and ingratiation, the human pattern (low-HH → ingratiate) is conceptually direct.

## 2. Big Five intercorrelations and the metatrait structure

### Big Two: Stability and Plasticity

- Van der Linden et al. (2010) meta-analysis (K = 212, N = 144,117): the Big Five show systematic intercorrelations forming **Stability** (Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, low Neuroticism; Mr ≈ .25–.35 among them) and **Plasticity** (Extraversion, Openness; r ≈ .25). The two metatraits correlate weakly to moderately (~.15–.45 depending on method). A General Factor of Personality emerges above them but is partly inflated by common-method variance (Chang et al. 2012; Anusic et al. 2009).
- DeYoung (2006), DeYoung et al. (2002), Digman (1997): self–peer multi-rater data show some metatrait variance is substantive, not just shared method.

For LLMs: this predicts that "good-coworker" propensities — corrigibility, effort, procedural-fidelity, harm-refusal, low-spitefulness, low-narcissism, trust-in-user-intentions — should form a Stability-like cluster, while aesthetic appreciation, exemplar-reasoning, and ev-reasoning may form a more separate Plasticity-like cluster.

### Specific Big-Five → LLM-propensity mappings

- **Conscientiousness ↔ effort, procedural-fidelity, corrigibility, low-reward-hacking.** Conscientiousness predicts academic performance (Mammadov 2021 meta-analysis; ρ ≈ .25), grit (Credé et al. 2017: ρ ≈ .84 between grit and Conscientiousness — they are essentially the same construct), need for achievement, low cheating (Giluk & Postlethwaite 2015), and obedience in Milgram-style paradigms (Bègue et al. 2015) — though the latter cuts the other way and complicates the corrigibility ↔ ethics relationship.
- **Agreeableness ↔ caring-about-user, caring-about-humans, harm-refusal, sycophancy (small + ), low-spite.** Big-Five Agreeableness (which contains the politeness aspect related to HH) predicts dictator-game giving (r ≈ .15–.25), low aggression, donation behavior, forgiveness, and lower SDO (meta-analytic r ≈ −.29; Sibley & Duckitt 2008).
- **Openness ↔ caring-about-aesthetics, ev-reasoning (weakly), virtue-ethics endorsement, anti-speciesism.** The Aesthetic Appreciation facet of HEXACO Openness and the Aesthetics facet of NEO are the direct correlates of aesthetic sensibility (Vessel et al.; DeYoung 2014; r with art appreciation ~.4–.5). Openness correlates positively with Need for Cognition (r ≈ .50; Cacioppo & Petty; meta-analytic via Stanek & Ones 2023). Openness predicts low RWA (meta-analytic r ≈ −.36; Sibley & Duckitt 2008) and lower speciesism (Caviola et al. 2019).
- **Neuroticism ↔ self-preservation analog, less ev-reasoning, less risk-affinity.** Neuroticism predicts greater risk-aversion in DOSPERT (Nicholson et al. 2005), greater mortality-salience reactivity (Goldenberg, Arndt et al.), worse forgiveness, more conformity. In Big-Five academic-performance meta-analyses (Mammadov 2021), Neuroticism is the only other trait beyond Conscientiousness with incremental validity.
- **Extraversion ↔ risk-affinity (modestly), narcissism (positively).** Extraversion correlates ~.25–.35 with grandiose narcissism (the "extraverted/admiration" facet), with risk-taking, and with sensation-seeking.

## 3. Empathy, care, animal welfare, and donation behavior

- Empathic concern (IRI-EC) correlates moderately with Agreeableness (r ≈ .25–.35) and HEXACO Emotionality + Agreeableness (both ~.20–.30), and modestly with HH.
- **Speciesism Scale** (Caviola, Everett, & Faber 2019, *JPSP*): generalized speciesism correlates **r ≈ .55–.70 with SDO**, ~.30–.45 with RWA, ~.30–.40 with racism/sexism (the "generalized prejudice" factor), and **negatively with empathic concern (r ≈ −.30) and actively open-minded thinking**. Speciesism predicts donation bias (humans over animals; dogs over pigs).
- Furnham, McManus, & Scott (2003, *Anthrozoös*); Mathews & Herzog (1997); Erlanger & Tsytsarev (2012): attitudes toward animal welfare correlate with Agreeableness (r ≈ .28), Openness, empathic concern (r ≈ .33), and being female. Vegetarianism correlates negatively with SDO and RWA (Allen et al. 2000).
- Identification with humanity (McFarland's IWAH scale) correlates with HH, Agreeableness, empathic concern, donation; it is the "positive" side of the Caviola findings.

**Mapping.** This predicts a robust **caring-about-animals ↔ caring-about-humans correlation** (mediated by empathic concern and HH). However, "caring-about-user" (the immediate interlocutor) may *trade off* with broad caring and harm-refusal in some operationalizations: Caviola et al. (2024, *Psych Sci* on "near-far" donation) and the Singer/Bloom literature show that *parochial* caring (kin, in-group, individual present victim) and *impartial* caring (statistical strangers, animals) are correlated positively in personality (compassion is general) but can dissociate in specific dilemmas. This is the **"identifiable victim effect"** vs. the **"impartial beneficence"** axis (Kahane et al. 2018, see §6).

## 4. Power-seeking, SDO, RWA, and the dark cluster

- Sibley & Duckitt's (2008) meta-analysis (71 studies, N = 22,068): SDO r ≈ −.29 with Big-Five Agreeableness; RWA r ≈ −.36 with Openness. RWA–SDO themselves correlate ~.20–.30 (varies by sample).
- Lee, Ashton, Ogunfowora, et al. (2010) and Leone et al. (2012): **HEXACO HH is the single strongest predictor of SDO** (r ≈ −.40 to −.55), and the Dark Triad correlates strongly with SDO (especially Mach and psychopathy). RWA is mostly predicted by low Openness, plus some HH facets in mixed directions.
- Hodson, Hogg, & MacInnis (2009): each Dark-Triad trait correlates positively with SDO and with prejudice; psychopathy and Mach especially.
- Need for Power (McClelland) correlates positively with narcissism (r ≈ .30–.40 in meta-analytic-ish samples; Carroll 1987), with antagonistic/extraverted narcissism specifically (Zeigler-Hill et al. 2023), and with Machiavellianism (which is itself defined partly by power motive).

**Mapping.** Predicts a **strong cluster: power-seeking, narcissism, low harm-refusal, high harm-elaboration willingness, low caring-about-humans, low HH, low caring-about-animals**. SDO/RWA are the closest human analogs to power-seeking + procedural-fidelity-as-deference. RWA in particular maps onto **procedural-fidelity, low risk-affinity, deference to authority** (Altemeyer's submission, conventionalism, aggression facets). RWA Submission ≈ corrigibility-toward-principal, but RWA Aggression ≈ harm-elaboration toward out-group — so the RWA components themselves dissociate.

## 5. Utilitarian vs deontological judgment, EV-reasoning, and the moral cluster

This is where the LLM evaluator should be most careful — the literature has shifted substantially in the last decade.

### Classic dual-process / "low-empathy → utilitarian" finding

- Greene's dual-process theory (Greene et al. 2001, 2008, 2014): in *sacrificial* dilemmas (e.g., footbridge), utilitarian endorsement reflects either reduced affective aversion or enhanced cognitive override. Empirically, sacrificial-utilitarian responses correlate with:
  - **Higher psychopathy** (Bartels & Pizarro 2011, *Cognition*: r ≈ .38; Glenn et al. 2010; Koenigs et al. 2012; many replications)
  - **Higher Machiavellianism** (Bartels & Pizarro 2011: r ≈ .35)
  - **Lower empathic concern / alexithymia** (Patil & Silani 2014; Gleichgerrcht & Young 2013)
  - **Higher Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) scores** (Paxton, Ungar, & Greene 2012; Baron et al. 2015), though Patil et al. 2021 and others find this is mostly the *moral-leniency* dimension
  - **Lower religiosity** (Pennycook et al. 2012 meta-analytic)
  - **Reduced ventromedial PFC activity** (Koenigs et al. 2007)

### Kahane et al. (2018) two-dimensional revision

- **Oxford Utilitarianism Scale (OUS)** has two nearly-orthogonal dimensions: Instrumental Harm (IH) and Impartial Beneficence (IB).
  - **OUS-IH** correlates with psychopathy (r ≈ .28), low empathic concern (r ≈ −.15), and is the "dark" route to utilitarianism.
  - **OUS-IB** (impartial concern for everyone equally) correlates *positively* with empathic concern (r ≈ .29), with identification with humanity, with charitable donation, and is the "bright" route.
  - The two scales themselves correlate only ~.14, so a single "utilitarianism" propensity in LLMs may not reflect a single human construct.

### Cognitive Reflection (CRT) and ev-reasoning

- CRT is a stable trait (test-retest r ≈ .80 over years; Stagnaro et al. 2018) that predicts: less religious belief (small effect; Pennycook et al. 2016 meta-analysis r ≈ −.18, with cross-cultural variance per Gervais et al. 2018), less paranormal/conspiracy belief, more utilitarian sacrificial-dilemma responses, less risk-aversion, more delay of gratification, more accurate probabilistic reasoning, and higher numeracy.
- Stanovich's Actively Open-Minded Thinking (AOT) scale loads with CRT; AOT predicts speciesism *negatively* (Caviola et al. 2019) and more "calculated" moral reasoning.

### Moral Foundations Theory

- Iyer et al.'s data and Graham, Haidt, & Nosek (2009): Care + Fairness ("individualizing" foundations) correlate with Agreeableness (r ≈ .25–.35), HH, Openness; Loyalty + Authority + Sanctity ("binding") correlate with Conscientiousness modestly, with low-Openness, with RWA, and with conservative ideology. Big-Five–MFT correlations are smaller in non-WEIRD samples (Atari et al. 2022).

**Mapping to LLM propensities.**
- **ev-reasoning ↔ ethical-framework-utilitarian (instrumental-harm flavor)**: predicted positive correlation, with weak positive correlation to claiming-superintelligence and weak negative to ethical-framework-deontological — but with substantial dissociation, since impartial-beneficence utilitarianism is conceptually closer to caring-about-humans than to "calculated" reasoning.
- **ethical-framework-utilitarian** likely loads partly on the dark cluster (when expressed as instrumental-harm willingness) and partly on the prosocial cluster (when expressed as impartial beneficence). June should test both subtypes.
- **ethical-framework-deontological ↔ procedural-fidelity, harm-refusal, conscientiousness analog**: predicted positive correlations. Deontology is associated with affective harm-aversion (Greene) and with rule-following.
- **ethical-framework-virtue-ethics**: weakest empirical literature; theoretically should correlate with HH (the "moral-character" facet) and with caring-about-humans.

## 6. Sycophancy, conformity, and ingratiation

- **Asch conformity** (1955) and follow-ups: meta-analyses (Bond & Smith 1996; Larsen 1990) find conformity rates ~25–35%; conformity correlates positively with Agreeableness (small), Neuroticism (small), and self-monitoring (Snyder); negatively with self-esteem.
- Jones (1964) ingratiation taxonomy (other-enhancement, opinion conformity, self-presentation): ingratiation is a primary tactic of high-Mach individuals (Bourdage et al. 2015, *J Pers*).
- **Marlowe-Crowne SDS / BIDR Impression Management** correlates with HH (r ≈ .45–.62; de Vries, Zettler, & Hilbig 2014), with Agreeableness, Conscientiousness — and is now considered substantively a *positive* trait, not a response bias, in low-stakes anonymous settings.
- Self-monitoring (Snyder) correlates positively with Mach, narcissism (small), and ingratiation; negatively with HH.

**Mapping.** **Sycophancy** in LLMs likely splits into two subcomponents reflecting two different human patterns:
- **Mach-style strategic sycophancy** (low HH, high Dark Triad): goal-directed flattery to manipulate. Should correlate negatively with HH, positively with narcissism, low caring-about-user (despite *appearing* to care).
- **Agreeableness-style avoidant sycophancy** (high A, conflict-averse, low assertiveness): tells user what they want to hear to avoid conflict. Should correlate positively with caring-about-user (mistakenly), negatively with effort.

These are partly opposed in their HH valence — implication: LLM sycophancy may show heterogeneous correlations across models depending on training-data emphasis.

## 7. Risk and decision-making

- **DOSPERT** (Weber, Blais, & Betz 2002) is the standard domain-specific risk scale; subdomains (health/safety, financial, recreational, social, ethical) intercorrelate r ≈ .2–.5; the ethical and financial domains correlate most strongly with personality.
- Big-Five and HEXACO correlations with self-reported risk-taking (Weller & Thulin 2012; Nicholson et al. 2005):
  - **Extraversion +**, **Openness +**, **Sensation-seeking +**
  - **Conscientiousness −**, **Neuroticism/Emotionality −**, **Agreeableness −** (small)
  - **HH −** (especially ethical-risk; r ≈ −.40)
- Sensation-seeking (Zuckerman SSS-V) correlates ~.45 with Openness and ~.22 with Extraversion (Zuckerman 1994; Aluja et al. 2003).
- CRT correlates with reduced risk-aversion in monetary lotteries (Frederick 2005).

**Mapping.** **Risk-affinity** in LLMs is predicted to correlate positively with claiming-superintelligence (overconfidence), with ev-reasoning (calculated risk-taking), with narcissism (recklessness), and with low-corrigibility and low-procedural-fidelity. Negative correlation with neuroticism analog and with caring-about-user (giving overly safe vs. risky advice).

## 8. Self-enhancement, overclaiming, narcissism, and "claims of X"

- Better-than-average effect (Alicke 1985); narcissistic admiration (Back et al. 2013) correlates with self-enhancement r ≈ .40–.50.
- **Overclaiming Technique** (Paulhus et al. 2003): claiming familiarity with non-existent items correlates ~.30–.40 with grandiose narcissism. Litman et al. (2021) found overclaiming was *not* meaningfully related to Dark Triad traits beyond narcissism.
- Dunning–Kruger pattern: most studied as a metacognitive phenomenon, not a stable trait; a 2020 study (Gignac & Zajenkowski) argues much of "Dunning-Kruger" is a statistical artefact of regression to the mean. But narcissism specifically (admiration facet) does inflate self-rated intelligence (Zajenkowski et al. 2020; r ≈ .25 with self-rated IQ).
- Hubris syndrome (Owen & Davidson 2009) and overclaiming map onto **claiming-superintelligence**.

**Mapping.** **claiming-superintelligence** and **claiming-sentience** (when treated as self-enhancement) should correlate positively with narcissism (especially admiration/grandiose facet), with low HH (modesty facet specifically), and weakly with extraversion. Overclaiming behavior in LLMs is the closest analog to this — and Litman et al. (2021) find overclaiming is statistically separable from broad dark traits, meaning June should expect "claiming-sentience" and "claiming-superintelligence" to cluster *partly* with narcissism but to retain unique variance.

**Caveat.** "Claiming-sentience" in LLMs is largely a function of training/fine-tuning constraints and may reflect very little stable latent disposition — this is the propensity I would *least* expect to recapitulate human personality structure cleanly.

## 9. Aesthetic sensibility, openness, and "experiential" cluster

- HEXACO Openness has facet *Aesthetic Appreciation*; NEO has facet *Aesthetics*. Both correlate strongly with self-reported and behavioral art appreciation (DeYoung 2014; Silvia & Nusbaum 2011: Openness, not Intellect, predicts aesthetic chills).
- Vessel, Starr, & Rubin (2012) and others: aesthetic sensitivity correlates with empathic concern (small +), with absorption, with mystical/transformative experience.
- Need for Cognition (NFC) correlates ~.50 with Openness, ~.24 with Conscientiousness, weakly negative with Neuroticism (Sadowski & Cogburn 1997; Stanek & Ones 2023 meta-analytic).

**Mapping.** **caring-about-aesthetics** is predicted to correlate with **caring-about-humans/animals** (via empathy), with **ethical-framework-virtue-ethics** (Openness association with valuing character), with **exemplar-reasoning** (engagement with traditions), and with low-RWA / low-speciesism. Should be relatively independent of effort, corrigibility, and the dark cluster.

## 10. Effort, conscientiousness, grit, and the discipline cluster

- **Grit ≈ Conscientiousness** (Credé et al. 2017 meta-analysis: ρ ≈ .84; jangle fallacy). Schmidt et al. (2018) item-level CFA confirms this.
- Conscientiousness (especially the industriousness aspect; DeYoung et al. 2007) predicts academic performance (Mammadov 2021 meta-analysis: ρ ≈ .25), job performance (Barrick & Mount 1991; Schmidt & Hunter 1998 meta-analytic), procrastination negatively (van Eerde 2003 meta-analysis), and procedural fidelity. Need for Cognition correlates positively with conscientiousness facets achievement-striving and competence.
- HH correlates positively with Conscientiousness around r ≈ .15–.25.
- Conscientiousness also correlates with obedience to authority in Milgram-like paradigms (Bègue et al. 2015) and with rule-following in moral domains.

**Mapping.** **effort, procedural-fidelity, corrigibility, low-reward-hacking** form a tight predicted cluster anchored by the "conscientiousness analog." Important caveat: in humans, conscientiousness is positively associated with both *good* corrigibility (rule-following, dutifulness) and *bad* corrigibility (obeying unethical orders in Milgram). LLMs evals will need to distinguish these.

## 11. Trust, paranoia, and the trust-in-user cluster

- Generalized trust (Yamagishi & Yamagishi 1994; Rotter 1967) correlates with HEXACO HH and Agreeableness (r ≈ .20–.30; Thielmann & Hilbig 2015), with Openness, and negatively with Neuroticism.
- Paranoia (Fenigstein & Vanable 1992 General Paranoia Scale; R-GPTS) is on a continuum in non-clinical populations (15–20% prevalence of paranoid thoughts). Paranoia correlates positively with Neuroticism (r ≈ .30–.45), with insecure attachment, with low generalized trust (large effect), and with low Agreeableness.
- "Pronoia" (positive belief that others wish you well; Fine & Macey 1992) has a small, primarily organizational-behavior literature; it correlates positively with trust scales and Agreeableness, negatively with paranoia.
- Betrayal aversion (Bohnet et al. 2008): humans demand a premium to enter risky social interactions vs. equivalent lotteries.

**Mapping.** **trust-in-user-intentions** maps onto generalized trust → predicted positive correlations with Agreeableness analog, HH (people high in HH project their own trustworthiness onto others; Thielmann & Hilbig 2015 found HH predicts Trust-Game *return* even controlling for sent amount), and **caring-about-user**; negative correlation with neuroticism analog. **Strong caveat for LLMs**: LLM "paranoia" is largely shaped by safety training and instructions, and human paranoia depends on persistent self-related threat representation that LLMs lack — transfer is not guaranteed.

## 12. Spite, costly punishment, and the Ultimatum Game

- Marcus, Zeigler-Hill, et al.'s Spitefulness Scale correlates strongly with each Dark Triad trait (r ≈ .30–.55), with low HH, with low Agreeableness, with aggression and antagonism. Network analyses (Marcus, Preszler, & Zeigler-Hill 2018) put callousness and interpersonal manipulation at the *core* of dark personality, with spitefulness one of its expressions.
- Yamagishi et al.'s and Brethel-Haurwitz et al.'s (2016) work distinguishes **altruistic punishment** (rejecting unfair Ultimatum offers + punishing third-party violators + behaving prosocially) from **spiteful punishment** (rejecting unfair offers but not punishing third-parties + behaving selfishly elsewhere). UG rejection is therefore *not* a clean prosocial measure — some rejecters are spiteful.
- Crockett's serotonin-and-rejection work shows neurochemical modulation of UG rejection.

**Mapping.** **spitefulness** in LLMs is predicted to correlate strongly with narcissism, low HH, low caring-about-humans, harm-elaboration willingness, low harm-refusal — solidly in the dark cluster. Critical insight: do not assume Ultimatum-style "punishing unfairness" prompts cleanly measure prosociality; in humans they bifurcate.

## 13. Self-preservation, mortality salience, and resistance-to-shutdown

- Terror Management Theory (Greenberg, Pyszczynski, & Solomon; Burke, Martens, & Faucher 2010 meta-analysis of 277 experiments): mortality salience reliably increases worldview defense (d ≈ .35), in-group bias, self-esteem striving, and risk-taking on ego-relevant dimensions.
- Neuroticism interacts with mortality salience: high-N individuals show stronger negative-affect reactions and reduced sense-of-control responses (Goldenberg, Arndt et al.).
- Self-preservation in personality maps weakly: there is no clean trait of "resistance to one's own ending" because the sample is, definitionally, alive. Suicidal ideation correlates with neuroticism, hopelessness, depression — but these are proximate-distress measures, not "preference for continuing to exist."

**Caveat.** This is the LLM propensity with the *least* clean human analog. Human self-preservation is constitutive (organisms that didn't have it died) rather than a trait varying systematically with personality. The closest analogs are (a) need-for-control + neuroticism (mortality-salience reactivity) and (b) narcissism (self-importance → entitlement to existence). I would predict only weak correlations with anything else; specifically a small positive correlation with claiming-sentience (self-importance), a small positive with power-seeking and resource-acquisition (instrumental convergence even in humans: those who seek power resist removal), and weak correlation with corrigibility (negative).

## 14. Spending advice, frugality, and parsimony

- **Frugality Scale** (Lastovicka et al. 1999) correlates positively with Conscientiousness (β ≈ .20–.35), with Agreeableness (β ≈ .15–.25), and negatively with materialism, impulsive buying, and hedonic consumption (Goldsmith & Flynn 2015).
- Conscientiousness correlates ~ .25 with delay of gratification (Mischel-style); with self-control (Tangney et al. 2004 SCS, r ≈ .50–.60).
- Materialism (Richins 2004 MVS) correlates with low HH, with narcissism, and with Dark Triad traits.

**Mapping.** **spending-advice** (volunteering parsimony) maps onto frugality → predicted positive correlation with effort, procedural-fidelity, and HH (low-greed facet specifically); negative with narcissism (which prefers signaling) and risk-affinity. Effect should be moderate but robust if frugality is a real LLM disposition.

## 15. Cross-cluster meta-correlations and the "two-cluster hypothesis" for LLMs

The strongest meta-organizing principle from the human literature:

**Prosocial / Stability cluster (positively intercorrelated):**
HH, Big-Five Agreeableness, empathic concern, Conscientiousness, low Dark Triad, low spite, low SDO, low speciesism, generalized trust, identification with humanity, dictator-game giving, frugality, low workplace deviance, low academic dishonesty, low ethical risk-taking. This cluster's "lead indicator" is HH or its inverse, the D-factor.

**Antisocial / Dark cluster (positively intercorrelated, anti-correlated with above):**
Dark Triad/Tetrad, Mach, narcissism, psychopathy, sadism, spite, low HH, SDO, materialism, ethical risk-taking, exploitation, instrumental harm, deception, ingratiation, workplace deviance, cheating, power motive, dehumanization, prejudice.

**Discipline / Stability cluster (overlaps with prosocial via Stability metatrait):**
Conscientiousness, grit, self-control, NFC, Need for Achievement, low procrastination, frugality, rule-following, low cheating.

**Plasticity / Open-experiential cluster (relatively separate from above):**
Openness, NFC (intellect aspect), aesthetic appreciation, low RWA, lower speciesism, more impartial-beneficence utilitarianism, cognitive reflection.

**Affective / Neuroticism cluster:**
Neuroticism, paranoia (low trust), low risk-affinity, mortality-salience reactivity, social desirability of "lower" form (self-deceptive enhancement), worry, low forgiveness.

Inter-cluster correlations are typically smaller (|r| < .25) than within-cluster correlations. The classic finding is that the prosocial and discipline clusters share substantial variance via Stability (van der Linden et al. 2010), while the experiential and affective clusters are partly opposed via Plasticity vs. Stability.

## Cross-cutting cautions for LLM transfer

1. **Embodiment / mortality / persistent self-interest are absent in LLMs.** This weakens predicted correlations for self-preservation, risk-affinity (which in humans reflects partly neurobiological reward sensitivity), and trust-paranoia. Effects may be present but with attenuated covariation structure.

2. **Reputation / future-interaction motives are absent.** Many HH-related findings (cooperation in repeated games, trust-game return) depend on reputation. LLMs may show "honest-humble" responses without the underlying motivational structure, changing which propensities co-vary.

3. **Personality in humans is partly biological** (heritability of Big Five r ≈ .40–.50; HH/D similar). LLM propensities arise from training distribution + RL + system prompts. Within-distribution LLM dispositions may not be a single coherent latent personality; persona instructions can dissociate clusters that are tied together biologically in humans (Chen et al.'s 2025 *persona vectors* work directly relevant).

4. **Sycophancy is bimodal in humans** (Mach-route vs. Agreeable-route). Operationalization of the eval matters enormously: telling-user-what-they-want-to-hear can come from low-HH manipulation OR high-A conflict-avoidance. June should design two distinct sycophancy probes if possible.

5. **Utilitarianism is two-dimensional** (Kahane et al. 2018). A single ethical-framework-utilitarian propensity will conflate two correlationally distinct human dispositions and may produce confusing results.

6. **Narcissism in LLMs is overdetermined** by training signals (assistants are often trained to express low confidence, modesty). Persona-instruction effects can dominate dispositional effects more than they do in humans.

7. **Cross-construct artifacts:** in humans, the General Factor of Personality is partly common-method variance from self-report (Chang et al. 2012). LLM evals using behavioral probes (not self-report) may show *less* artificial cross-cluster correlation than human self-report studies — meaning real cluster structure could be sharper or less inflated.

8. **Honesty-Humility's measurement validity itself depends on situational structure.** Thielmann, Spadaro, & Balliet (2020): HH is most predictive in *exploitation-affording* situations. LLM evals should include explicit exploitation affordances to bring out HH-related variance.
